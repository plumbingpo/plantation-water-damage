Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Plantation

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Set Drying Equipment

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.
  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.
  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
