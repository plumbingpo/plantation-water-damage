Wet Carpet Drying: What Plantation Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. Our team in Plantation extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

Signs to Look For

  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • A clammy, cool patch underfoot that lingers well past the original spill
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Soaked Pad Underneath

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Mold Under the Carpet

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.
