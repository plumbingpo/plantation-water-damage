A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. Day or night, 24/7, Plantation Restoration sends flood cleanup crews throughout Plantation to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. With the standing water gone, our IICRC-certified technicians check the contamination level and apply the right sanitizing steps, including antimicrobial treatment, HEPA air filtration and disinfection of every affected surface. Materials beyond saving, like contaminated drywall, damaged insulation and saturated carpet pad, are removed and disposed of the right way.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm
  • Coat basement walls with waterproofing and seal any cracks in the foundation

The Benefits of Professional Flood Cleanup

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

When to Call for Flood Cleanup in Plantation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • A sewer or drain backup pushing contaminated water into living spaces
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Receding water that leaves muddy residue and debris behind
